## Completed behavior group

Type-argument free-function explicit specialization, including explicit and
omitted declarator arguments, canonical full declared-type deduction and
mutual partial ordering of named templates. The selected signature keeps its
primary/argument key and ABI identity, but takes its own parsed parameters,
definition, inline/constexpr/deletion and no-inline facts. Exception agreement
precedes rejection of function default arguments. A normally defined
specialization of a deleted primary is callable; an explicitly deleted one
remains deleted. Failed introducers restore context.

An explicit specialization suppresses queued and subsequent implicit primary
body demand. Its own ordinary body is checked without replaying the parser or
copying the primary body. Non-inline definitions emit strongly at the source
boundary; inline definitions retain their own inline hint and emit on demand.
Late declarations replace the primary shell for subsequent uses, as the frozen
implementation permits before body instantiation.

Explicit instantiation **after** an explicit specialization only records the
non-inline object-root fact: repeated definitions and later extern declarations
have no suppression/duplicate effect. An instantiation **before** specialization
retains its queued emission demand even if the later specialization is inline,
but then has no object-root flag. These two orders are observably different in
the oracle and are preserved.

Three Rust ownership tests check canonical reuse without primary-body copying,
error-context restoration and both explicit-instantiation orders. Fixed-manifest
coverage and 26 protected adapter probes validate declarations, late uses,
renamed/cv/reference parameters, overload ordering, operator specialization,
qualified namespaces, recursion, inherited call defaults, constexpr/inline
ownership, deleted primaries, exception agreement/rejection and duplicate/no
matching definitions. No fixture, oracle or protected policy was edited.

## Remaining boundary and handoff

Next related owner: explicit **class** specialization and its definition-state
ownership (`specialized_class` / `class_explicit_specialization`), followed by
explicit class-instance member specialization. Class shells must select the
explicit definition before demanding the primary layout/members; completing a
class must not instantiate unrelated member bodies. Thirteen current cases
stop at class-template specialization and two at the explicit member-function
boundary. Existing destructor/static duplicate paths gained cases through
shared declaration handling; those gains do not certify their entire families.

The largest independent boundary is non-type template parameter binding
(100 cases). It requires typed type/value/pack argument identities through
canonical keys, defaults, substitution, constant bindings and ABI emission;
do not disguise constants as textual types. Remaining packs need parameter
collection, partition-preserving arguments, expansion contexts, sizeof...,
call/body/base/member/initializer expansion and demand-aware copying. Other
remaining groups include variable templates, dependent names, literal
operators, exception constant evaluation, static data specialization, constant
arithmetic diagnostics and existing LowIR differences. PA35 is not active.

Independent audit questions: verify exact signature/type/prototype provenance
across repeated declarations; both instantiation/specialization orders and
inline roots; queued implicit demand suppression without broader body demand;
exception/default ordering and context restoration; source naming order and
unchanged per-function IR lifetimes. Ralph owns checkpoint/final audits,
last-audited commit and stage advancement.
